About China Compass

Methodology, sources, and principles behind the Canada-China intelligence briefing

Mission

China Compass provides daily, bilingual intelligence briefings on the Canada-China relationship. Our goal is to make complex geopolitical dynamics accessible to Canadian policymakers, business professionals, journalists, researchers, and the general public.

We believe that an informed public is essential for a healthy democracy navigating complex relationships with authoritarian states. China Compass aims to be a trusted, non-partisan resource that cuts through noise and ideology to present factual, contextualized analysis.

Methodology

Signal Collection

Signals are collected daily from a curated set of open sources including:

  • Canadian government statements and press releases (Global Affairs Canada, PMO, parliamentary records)
  • Chinese government sources (Xinhua, State Council, MOFCOM, MFA spokesperson transcripts)
  • Wire services (Reuters, AP, AFP)
  • Specialist China analysis (Sinocism, China Brief, MERICS)
  • Canadian parliamentary Hansard and committee transcripts
  • Trade data (Statistics Canada, China Customs)

Severity Rating

Each signal is assigned a severity rating on a five-point scale:

  • Critical: Immediate, high-impact events requiring urgent attention (e.g., detention of Canadian citizens, military confrontation)
  • High: Significant developments with direct bilateral impact (e.g., trade bans, ambassador summoning)
  • Elevated: Notable developments with potential to escalate (e.g., tariff extensions, regulatory changes)
  • Moderate: Developments worth monitoring with indirect implications (e.g., domestic policy changes, legislative updates)
  • Low: Background developments for context and awareness (e.g., social trends, academic reports)

Tension Index

The Canada-China Tension Index is a composite score (0-10) calculated from six components:

Component Weight Description
Diplomatic 25% Embassy actions, statements, summoning events
Trade 25% Tariffs, bans, trade volume changes, disputes
Military 15% PLA activities, NORAD incidents, defense posture
Political 15% Legislative actions, party congress outcomes, foreign interference
Technology 10% Tech bans, IP disputes, research restrictions
Social 10% Diaspora issues, student concerns, public sentiment

Each component is scored 0-10 based on the severity and frequency of signals in a 30-day rolling window. The composite is a weighted average.

Category System

Signals are categorized into eight domains:

  • Diplomatic: Bilateral relations, embassy actions, consular affairs, and diplomatic signaling
  • Trade: Import/export flows, tariffs, sanctions, trade agreements, and commercial disputes
  • Military: Defense activities, naval operations, airspace incidents, and security cooperation
  • Technology: Tech policy, research collaboration, IP disputes, and digital infrastructure
  • Political: Legislative actions, elections, party politics, and foreign interference
  • Economic: Macroeconomic trends, investment flows, financial markets, and fiscal policy
  • Social: Diaspora communities, education, cultural exchange, and public opinion
  • Legal: Extradition, rule of law, judicial proceedings, and regulatory enforcement

Bilingual Approach

Content is produced in both English and Chinese to serve Canada's bilingual China-watching community. Chinese-language sources are analyzed in the original language. Entity names include both English and Chinese characters. The glossary system provides inline translations of key terms.

Editorial Principles

  1. Factual accuracy: Claims are sourced and verifiable. We distinguish between confirmed facts, official statements, and analysis.
  2. Non-partisan: We do not advocate for any political position on Canada-China relations. We inform; readers decide.
  3. Transparency: Our methodology is public. Our sources are cited. Our limitations are acknowledged.
  4. Accessibility: Complex geopolitics should be understandable to non-specialists. We avoid jargon and explain context.
  5. Bilingual equity: Both language versions receive equal editorial attention. Neither is a mere translation of the other.

Limitations

We acknowledge the following limitations:

  • China Compass relies on open-source intelligence. We do not have access to classified information.
  • Chinese government transparency is limited. Some assessments involve informed inference.
  • The Tension Index is a simplified model. Reality is more nuanced than any single number.
  • Market data is delayed and indicative. It should not be used as the basis for investment decisions.
  • We are a small team. Coverage gaps are inevitable.
